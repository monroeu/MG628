1. What Descriptive Analytics Does

Descriptive analytics answers “What happened?” by summarizing historical data so managers can see patterns, trends, anomalies, and operational baselines before making decisions.

Teaching Note — Description comes before explanation

A descriptive statistic summarizes what is in front of you. It does not, by itself, explain why the result occurred or prove what will happen next. A manager should first establish a reliable baseline, then ask whether the pattern needs diagnosis, prediction, or action.

QuestionAnalytics typeExample
What happened?DescriptiveAverage wait time last quarter was 34 minutes.
Why did it happen?DiagnosticWait time increased mainly during understaffed evening shifts.
What may happen next?PredictiveForecast next month’s patient volume.
What should we do?PrescriptiveRecommend a staffing schedule that meets a service target.
Managerial habit: Before acting on a summary, ask: What population and time period does this describe? Is the data complete? Is the statistic appropriate for the shape of the data?
Managerial frame: descriptive analytics is the first layer of analysis. It summarizes observed performance before a manager moves to prediction or prescription.

2. Central Tendency — Mean, Median & Mode

Central tendency describes the typical or central result. Mean, median, and mode can tell different stories, especially when extreme values are present.

Teaching Note — Mean, Median, and Mode answer different versions of “typical”

Mean — arithmetic average

Mean = sum of all values ÷ number of values

Use it when: numerical data are reasonably balanced and every observation should contribute to the center.

Good examples: average units sold per transaction; average admissions per day when there are no extreme surge days.

Watch out: one very large or very small value can pull the mean toward the extreme.

Median — middle observation

Median = middle value after sorting

Use it when: the distribution is skewed or contains extreme values.

Good examples: household income, hospital length of stay, patient cost, home price, or wait time.

Why managers use it: it answers, “What does the middle case look like?” without allowing a few extremes to dominate.

Mode — most frequent value

Mode = value or category occurring most often

Use it when: the most common category, product, visit hour, diagnosis type, or service request matters.

Good examples: most common product purchased; most common ER arrival hour.

Watch out: a dataset may have no unique mode or may have multiple modes.
Why the choice matters: Suppose five service times are 45, 48, 50, 52, and 205 minutes. The mean is 80 minutes, while the median is 50 minutes. If you describe the “typical” experience only with the mean, one extreme delay makes the entire operation appear much slower than the experience of the middle case.
MBA: Median customer order value may be more representative when a few very large corporate purchases inflate the mean.
MPA: Median household income is often more representative than mean income when a small number of very high-income households are present.
Healthcare: Median wait time or length of stay may be more useful when a few complex cases require exceptionally long care.
Decision rule: Do not ask “Which statistic is best?” in the abstract. Ask “Which statistic best represents the managerial question and the distribution I have?”
Three ways to describe the center
df["Unit Price"].mean()
df["Unit Price"].median()
df["Unit Price"].mode()
Read it in English: calculate the average, middle value, and most frequent value for Unit Price.

3. Dispersion — Range, Variance, Standard Deviation & IQR

Dispersion measures consistency and risk. Two locations can have the same average performance but very different variability.

Teaching Note — Center without spread can be misleading

Two departments can have the same mean but very different consistency. Dispersion tells a manager whether performance is stable, volatile, tightly controlled, or unpredictable.

MeasureWhat it tells youBest useLimitation / caution
RangeTotal span from minimum to maximumFast sense of extremesUses only two observations and is highly sensitive to outliers
VarianceAverage squared spread around the meanStatistical calculations and modelingMeasured in squared units, so it is less intuitive for managers
Standard deviationTypical spread around the meanConsistency, volatility, process stabilityMost interpretable when the mean is meaningful; sensitive to extremes
IQRSpread of the middle 50% (Q3 − Q1)Skewed data and robust outlier screeningIntentionally ignores the outer 50% of observations
Same mean, different risk: Clinic A may average 20 minutes with most patients between 18–22 minutes. Clinic B may also average 20 minutes but alternate between 5 and 35 minutes. The same mean does not imply the same patient experience. Standard deviation exposes the difference.
Business: High standard deviation in delivery time means customers face uncertainty even if average delivery time looks acceptable.
Public Administration: Similar average service-processing time across districts can hide much greater inconsistency in one district.
Healthcare: Variability in cost or length of stay may signal differences in patient acuity, workflow, or practice patterns.
Technical note: pandas .std() and Excel STDEV.S use the sample standard deviation by default. That matches the Week 5 assignment’s sample-statistics framing.
MeasureMeaningManagerial use
RangeMax − MinOverall span
VarianceSquared spreadAnalytical variability
Standard deviationTypical spread around meanConsistency / volatility
IQRQ3 − Q1Robust spread / outlier screening

4. Frequency Analysis & Histograms

Frequency tables count how often values or categories occur. Histograms group numerical values into bins to reveal the shape and concentration of a distribution.

Teaching Note — Frequency answers “how often?” and histograms answer “how is it distributed?”

A frequency table is usually the first place to look when you want to understand prevalence. It can count categories such as Product or Department, or numerical values after they are grouped.

ToolUse whenManagerial question
Frequency tableYou need exact counts or proportionsWhich product, service, or category occurs most often?
Bar chartYou are comparing categoriesWhich region has the most revenue? Which department has the most billing?
HistogramYou are examining a numerical distributionAre prices tightly clustered, spread out, skewed, or multi-peaked?
Bins matter: A histogram groups numerical values into intervals. Too few bins can hide important structure; too many can make normal random variation look meaningful. The goal is to reveal the overall shape without overinterpreting noise.
Common mistake: A histogram is not a bar chart. Bars in a histogram represent adjacent numerical intervals; bars in a categorical bar chart represent distinct categories.
Teaching question: Before choosing a chart, ask whether your x-axis variable is categorical or numerical.
Frequency and distribution
df["Product"].value_counts()
plt.hist(df["Unit Price"], bins=15)
Read it in English: count categories, then group numerical values into intervals to see the distribution.

5. Distribution Shape — Normality, Skewness & Kurtosis

Distribution shape adds context beyond the average. Skewness describes asymmetry; kurtosis describes tail behavior and the tendency to produce extreme observations.

Teaching Note — Shape explains why the center can behave the way it does

ConceptInterpretationManagerial implication
Normal / symmetricLeft and right sides are balanced; mean and median are similarMean and standard deviation often summarize the distribution well
Positive (right) skewA few high values create a long right tail; mean often exceeds medianAverage may overstate the experience of the typical case
Negative (left) skewA few low values create a long left tail; mean often falls below medianAverage may understate the typical case
KurtosisDescribes tail heaviness / concentration of extreme valuesHigher tail risk can matter even when mean and standard deviation look acceptable

The 68–95–99.7 rule

For data that are approximately normal, about 68% of observations fall within 1 standard deviation of the mean, about 95% within 2, and about 99.7% within 3. This is a useful benchmark, not permission to assume every dataset is normal.

Business: Revenue can be right-skewed when a small number of very large customers account for much of total sales.
MPA: Household income is often right-skewed, which is why median income is frequently preferred for policy baselines.
Healthcare: Cost and length-of-stay data can be right-skewed because a small number of complex cases generate very high resource use.
Python note: pandas .kurt() reports excess kurtosis, where a normal distribution is approximately 0. Focus on interpretation rather than memorizing a cutoff.
Decision rule: Compare the histogram, mean, median, skewness, and outliers together. No single shape statistic should be interpreted in isolation.
ShapeInterpretation
SymmetricLeft and right sides are broadly balanced.
Positive skewLonger right tail; a few high observations can pull the mean upward.
Negative skewLonger left tail; a few low observations can pull the mean downward.

7. Python Descriptive Statistics

Use pandas and NumPy to calculate summary statistics reproducibly. The goal is not merely to produce numbers, but to interpret what those numbers mean for a manager.

Teaching Note — Code produces numbers; analysis gives them meaning

df.describe() is an efficient first look at numerical data. It reports count, mean, standard deviation, minimum, quartiles, and maximum. It does not automatically give the mode, and it does not explain whether any result is managerially important.

OutputQuestion to ask
countAre values missing? Does every variable have the same number of observations?
meanIs the average representative, or is it being pulled by extreme values?
stdHow consistent or volatile are the observations?
min / maxAre the extremes plausible, or do they need investigation?
25% / 50% / 75%Where do the middle half and median of the distribution fall?
Missing-data teaching moment: pandas calculations generally ignore NaN values. Therefore, always inspect count before comparing statistics across variables; two means may be based on different numbers of records.
Good notebook practice: Follow a numerical result with a Markdown sentence that interprets it. Do not submit a table of numbers without explaining what changed, what varies, and what a manager should notice.
Build the assignment summary
summary = pd.DataFrame({
    "Count": df[numeric_columns].count(),
    "Mean": df[numeric_columns].mean(),
    "Median": df[numeric_columns].median(),
    "Mode": df[numeric_columns].mode().iloc[0],
    "Std Dev": df[numeric_columns].std(),
    "Minimum": df[numeric_columns].min(),
    "Maximum": df[numeric_columns].max()
})

8. Visualization & Outlier Detection

Bar charts compare categories, histograms show numerical distributions, and box plots help identify unusual values. The IQR rule provides a repeatable way to flag potential outliers.

Teaching Note — Visualization and outlier detection are interpretation tools

VisualBest forQuestion it answers
Bar chartComparing categoriesWhich product, region, department, or facility is larger?
HistogramNumerical distributionWhere are values concentrated and is the distribution skewed?
Box plotCenter, spread, and potential outliersAre some observations unusually far from the middle 50%?

The IQR rule

Lower fence = Q1 − 1.5 × IQR    |    Upper fence = Q3 + 1.5 × IQR

Values beyond these fences are potential outliers. The rule flags cases for review; it does not prove that the values are wrong.

Healthcare: A very high billing record could be a data-entry error—or a legitimate high-acuity surgical case. Deleting it without investigation could remove the patients who matter most for resource planning.
Business: A very large sale could be fraud, a bulk corporate order, or a strategic account. Each interpretation leads to a different management response.
MPA: Unusually high spending in one district might indicate waste, but it could also reflect a disaster response or a population with greater need.
Outlier workflow: Flag → verify source → understand context → document decision → retain, correct, or exclude with justification.
IQR outlier rule
Q1 = df[column].quantile(0.25)
Q3 = df[column].quantile(0.75)
IQR = Q3 - Q1
lower = Q1 - 1.5 * IQR
upper = Q3 + 1.5 * IQR
Important: a flagged outlier is a case to investigate, not automatically a mistake to delete.

11. Discussion Preparation — Reliability, Ethics, Validity & Moving Forward

Use descriptive statistics as a manager, then question whether the numbers are reliable, ethical, and valid. Prepare to explain what additional data would be needed before moving toward prediction.

Discussion teaching moment — Reliability, validity, ethics, and the limits of averages

Reliability asks whether the measurement is consistent. Validity asks whether the measure actually represents the concept you claim to measure. A statistic can be calculated correctly and still be a poor basis for a decision if the underlying measure is unreliable or invalid.

Reliability example: If different offices record service time differently, the average may not be comparable across locations.
Validity example: Total revenue may be valid for measuring sales volume but invalid as a stand-alone measure of profitability.
Ethical question: Who disappears inside the average? A citywide average, hospital average, or company-wide KPI can conceal groups receiving substantially worse outcomes.
Moving forward: Descriptive analytics tells you what the dataset shows. Before moving to prediction, ask what external drivers, context, and omitted variables you still need.
ContextQuestion to ask
ReliabilityIf a city dataset excludes residents without internet access, is the reported average representative?
EthicsIf clinics are judged by average treatment time, could complex-patient clinics be unfairly penalized?
ValidityDoes high total revenue imply success if nearly all revenue comes from one product?
Moving forwardWhat additional outside data would you need before forecasting future volume, cost, or time?
