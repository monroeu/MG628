1. Why Data Wrangling Matters

Data wrangling transforms raw, messy data into a trusted analytical foundation. Cleaning is one part of the broader journey from discovery through publishing.

Data wrangling

The end-to-end process of discovering, structuring, cleaning, enriching, validating, and publishing data for analysis.

Data cleaning

The focused work of detecting, correcting, or removing errors, inconsistencies, duplicates, and inaccurate values.

Why managers care

Clean evidence supports better decisions, faster analysis, more reliable reporting, and stronger compliance.

Big data context

Volume, velocity, and variety increase the cost of weak data preparation because errors can scale just as quickly as insights.

2. Data Quality Problems & Cleaning Decisions

Good cleaning is not mechanical. Each change can alter the meaning of the dataset, so the analyst must understand the risk before applying a rule.

Missing values

Decide whether to impute, label, investigate, or exclude. The correct choice depends on why the value is missing.

Duplicates

Repeated records can overstate counts, revenue, utilization, and demand if they are not identified correctly.

Inconsistencies

Different labels, formats, units, dates, currencies, or categories can split one real-world concept into several false groups.

Outliers & errors

An unusual value may be a mistake, a rare but real event, or a warning signal. Investigate before removing it.

Common pitfall: over-cleaning. A smooth dataset is not automatically a truthful dataset. Document each rule and preserve context.

4. Healthcare Case: Patient Data Quality

The healthcare practice dataset intentionally contains mixed gender labels, missing discharge reasons, a duplicate record, and a logically impossible date sequence.

Download healthcare_data.csvDownload Colab notebook
healthcare_clean = healthcare.drop_duplicates().copy() gender_map = {'M':'Male','F':'Female','Male':'Male','Female':'Female'} healthcare_clean['Gender'] = healthcare_clean['Gender'].map(gender_map) healthcare_clean['Discharge_Reason'] = healthcare_clean['Discharge_Reason'].fillna('Standard') healthcare_clean['Date_Error'] = healthcare_clean['Discharge_Date'] < healthcare_clean['Admission_Date']

The source exercise expects six unique rows after deduplication, standardized gender labels, and a True date-error flag for patient P003. Valid records are then used to calculate average treatment cost by discharge reason.

6. Ethics, Reliability & Managerial Judgment

Cleaning rules are decisions about people and operations, not just technical operations. Missingness, category definitions, and outlier treatment can change who is represented and what managers conclude.

Missingness → Reliability

If missing records are concentrated among certain populations, deleting them may systematically weaken the dataset.

Categories → Validity

Combining labels that are not truly equivalent can create a clean-looking but conceptually invalid comparison.

Outliers → Ethics

A rare case may represent a data error, but it may also represent a vulnerable group or an operational warning.

Managerial standard

Ask what was changed, why it was changed, who could be excluded, and whether the cleaned dataset still represents the decision problem.

Answer

A rare value may represent a vulnerable population, emerging risk, fraud signal, safety event, or true exception—not noise.

Answer

Excessive smoothing or deletion can remove the very variation managers need to understand.
