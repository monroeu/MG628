1. Wrangling vs. Cleaning — Understand the Workflow Before Editing

Data cleaning is one part of a broader data-wrangling process. Before changing a value, a manager should understand where the data came from, what problem is present, which rule is defensible, and how the result will be validated and published.

Week 4 framing: Wrangling is the end-to-end journey from raw data to a trusted dataset. Cleaning is the phase where you resolve quality problems such as missing values, duplicates, and inconsistent labels.

The six-step wrangling workflow

1 · DiscoveryProfile variables, types, gaps, duplicates, and anomalies.
2 · StructuringReshape and type data so analysis can work correctly.
3 · CleaningDeduplicate, impute, standardize, and correct documented errors.
4 · EnrichingAdd useful fields, conversions, or merged information.
5 · ValidatingApply business rules and reconcile totals.
6 · PublishingDeliver the trusted output to a report, BI tool, warehouse, or stakeholder.

Why the data environment matters: the 3 Vs

Volume

Large amounts of data can exceed what manual spreadsheet cleaning can handle reliably.

Velocity

Fast-arriving data increases the value of repeatable cleaning rules that can run again without rebuilding the process.

Variety

Different formats, systems, and coding conventions create more opportunities for inconsistent labels, types, and units.

Data-quality audit: what are you looking for?

Missing values
Blank Amount, Customer_Status, or Discharge_Reason fields.
Duplicates
The same transaction or patient record appearing more than once.
Inconsistencies
NE vs North East, M/F vs Male/Female, mixed date formats.
Typos / errors
Incorrect labels, malformed values, or impossible dates.
Outliers
Unusual values that may be valid, erroneous, or operationally important.
Irrelevant data
Fields or records that add noise without supporting the decision.
Managerial rule: do not clean because a value looks unusual. Clean because you can document the problem, the decision rule, the business rationale, and the validation check.

Retail consequence

The raw retail amount totals $995.50. After removing the duplicate T1001 and converting £200 at 1.25 USD/GBP, the validated total is $895.50.

2. Excel & Power Query — Profile, Clean, and Preserve an Audit Trail

Power Query is the professional Excel cleaning path because every transformation appears in Applied Steps. The raw CSV remains untouched, and next month's file can be cleaned again with Refresh.

Import. Data → Get Data → From File → From Text/CSV → choose retail_sales_data.csv.
Transform. Confirm comma delimiter and UTF-8, then choose Transform Data.
Profile. View → Column quality + Column distribution.
Read the evidence. Amount and Customer_Status have blanks; Region has five labels for three business regions.
CleaningContext before you run this exercise
SituationThe raw retail file contains T1001 twice. If both rows remain, the same $150 sale is counted twice.
Your jobUse a reproducible deduplication step rather than manually deleting a row.
Checkpoint7 raw rows become 6; one exact T1001 remains.
Why it mattersDuplicate revenue can overstate sales, distort regional performance, and trigger bad inventory or incentive decisions.
Power Query records the duplicate-removal step
= Table.Distinct(#"Changed Type")
Read it in English: Take the table produced by the previous step named Changed Type and keep each distinct row only once.
Table.Distinct
Returns unique rows.
#"Changed Type"
The output of the previous Applied Step.
What you should expect: The repeated T1001 disappears and the retail file goes from 7 rows to 6.
Why a manager cares: Duplicate transactions overstate revenue and can drive inventory or performance decisions using sales that never happened.
CleaningContext before you run this exercise
SituationThe business has three real regions, but the source uses labels such as NE and North East for the same place.
Your jobMap alternate labels to one approved reporting label.
CheckpointNE becomes North East; the final report contains three regions, not five.
Why it mattersFragmented labels split one market into multiple categories and understate the true regional total.
Power Query standardizes a Region label
= Table.ReplaceValue(#"Removed Duplicates",
    "NE", "North East",
    Replacer.ReplaceValue, {"Region"})
Read it in English: Starting with the deduplicated table, replace the entire Region value NE with North East, only in the Region column.
"NE"
The raw label.
"North East"
The approved label.
Replacer.ReplaceValue
Match the whole cell value rather than a substring.
What you should expect: NE and North East now belong to one category.
Why a manager cares: Without standardization, the same market appears as two regions and its performance is fragmented.
EnrichingContext before you run this exercise
SituationT1003 is recorded as 200 GBP while the other transactions are in USD. Adding the raw Amount column would mix units.
Your jobCreate a common-currency field using the exercise assumption 1 GBP = 1.25 USD.
CheckpointT1003 becomes $250.00; USD rows pass through unchanged.
Why it mattersManagers can compare regions only after monetary values share a common unit and the exchange-rate assumption is documented.
Custom column: convert GBP to USD
if [Currency] = "GBP" then [Amount] * 1.25 else [Amount]
Read it in English: For the current row, if Currency is GBP, multiply Amount by 1.25; otherwise keep the original Amount.
if ... then ... else
Power Query's conditional decision structure.
[Currency]
The current row's Currency field.
[Amount] * 1.25
The exercise's documented GBP-to-USD conversion.
What you should expect: T1003 changes from 200 GBP to 250 USD; USD rows remain unchanged.
Why a manager cares: Adding currencies without conversion compares different units as though they were the same.
ValidatingContext before you run this exercise
SituationT1005 has no Amount. A blank sale value is a data-quality issue, but the dataset gives no defensible amount to substitute.
Your jobCreate a TRUE/FALSE flag instead of inventing revenue.
CheckpointT1005 is flagged TRUE and remains visible for follow-up.
Why it mattersA zero or average would make a claim the source did not support; a flag preserves the uncertainty and audit trail.
Custom column: flag the missing amount
[Amount] = null
Read it in English: Ask whether Amount is missing in this row and return a logical TRUE/FALSE value.
What you should expect: T1005 is TRUE; the other rows are FALSE.
Why a manager cares: A validation flag makes missing revenue visible without inventing a number.

If Power Query is unavailable, helper columns make one cleaning decision at a time. This approach is transparent because you can inspect the original field and the cleaned result side by side.

CleaningContext before you run this exercise

Your jobUse a controlled mapping table so every known raw label resolves to an approved clean label and unexpected labels surface as UNMAPPED.

4. SQL — Create the Raw Table, Load the Mess, and Profile It

SQL cleans data where it already lives: inside the database. For this class you can use Jam SQL Studio in SQLite browser mode. The SQL below is designed to run in SQLite and MySQL 8 for this lesson.

StructuringContext before you run this exercise
SituationA database needs an explicit schema before it can reliably sort dates, calculate money, or distinguish missing values from text.
Your jobDefine column names and data types that reflect the meaning of each field.
CheckpointThe empty retail_raw table has typed columns ready to receive the source rows.
Why it mattersTypes are a data-quality contract: they reduce ambiguity before downstream cleaning begins.
Create the raw retail table
CREATE TABLE retail_raw (
  Transaction_ID  VARCHAR(10),
  Date            DATE,
  Region          VARCHAR(30),
  Store_Type      VARCHAR(20),
  Amount          DECIMAL(10,2),
  Currency        CHAR(3),
  Customer_Status VARCHAR(15)
);
Read it in English: Create an empty table named retail_raw and define the fields that every raw transaction is expected to contain.
CREATE TABLE
Create a new database table.
VARCHAR
Variable-length text.
DATE
A date field rather than arbitrary text.
DECIMAL(10,2)
Money-like numeric precision with two decimal places.
CHAR(3)
Three-character currency code such as USD or GBP.
Why a manager cares: A table schema is a data contract. Types document what the organization expects each field to mean.
Discovery / GovernanceContext before you run this exercise
SituationThe purpose of a raw layer is fidelity to the source, not cleanliness. The duplicate, blanks, alternate labels, and GBP value must remain visible.
Your jobInsert the raw rows exactly as received and clean in a separate layer.
Checkpointretail_raw contains all 7 source rows, including T1001 twice and NULL values.
Why it mattersKeeping raw data untouched allows every cleaning decision to be reproduced, challenged, or reversed.
Load the raw rows — including the problems

('T1001','2023-05-01','North East','Online',150.00,'USD','Gold'),
('T1005','2023-05-03','S. Region','Online',NULL,'USD','Silver'),
('T1006','2023-05-03','West Coast','Online',300.00,'USD','Platinum');
Read it in English: Insert the seven source rows exactly as received—including the duplicate, inconsistent labels, GBP value, and missing values.
INSERT INTO
Add rows to a table.
NULL
Missing/unknown value; it is not zero and not the text "NULL".
Why a manager cares: Preserve the raw source. Cleaning should happen in a separate layer so every decision can be audited.
DiscoveryContext before you run this exercise
SituationBefore filling or deleting anything, you need to know how many values are actually missing and where.
Your jobProfile completeness with row counts and NULL-aware counts.
Checkpoint7 total rows, 6 present Amount values, 1 missing Amount, 1 missing status.
Why it mattersThe size and pattern of missingness should influence the treatment; cleaning without profiling can hide the scope of the problem.
Q1 — How complete are Amount and Customer_Status?
SELECT COUNT(*) AS total_rows,
       COUNT(Amount) AS amount_present,
       COUNT(*) - COUNT(Amount) AS amount_missing,
       SUM(CASE WHEN Customer_Status IS NULL
                 OR TRIM(Customer_Status) = ''
                THEN 1 ELSE 0 END) AS status_missing
FROM retail_raw;
Read it in English: Count all rows, count rows where Amount is present, subtract to find missing Amounts, then count rows whose status is null or blank after trimming spaces.
COUNT(*)
Counts every row.
COUNT(Amount)
Counts only non-NULL Amount values.
CASE WHEN
SQL's conditional decision structure.
TRIM()
Removes leading/trailing spaces before testing for empty text.
Expected: total_rows 7 · amount_present 6 · amount_missing 1 · status_missing 1.
DiscoveryContext before you run this exercise
SituationA dataset can look reasonable row by row while still containing fragmented categories and repeated business keys.
Your jobGroup values to expose label variants, then use HAVING to find IDs that appear more than once.
CheckpointFive raw Region labels appear; T1001 has two copies.
Why it mattersDiscovery queries establish evidence for the cleaning rules instead of relying on guesswork.
Q2 and Q3 — Find label variants and repeated IDs
SELECT Region, COUNT(*) AS n
FROM retail_raw
GROUP BY Region
ORDER BY Region;

SELECT Transaction_ID, COUNT(*) AS copies
FROM retail_raw
GROUP BY Transaction_ID
HAVING COUNT(*) > 1;
Read it in English: First, collapse identical Region labels into groups and count them. Then group by transaction ID and keep only groups with more than one row.
GROUP BY
Creates groups of rows sharing the same value.
HAVING
Filters groups after grouping; this is different from WHERE, which filters rows before grouping.
Expected: five Region spellings appear; T1001 has 2 copies.

5. SQL — Clean with a View, Validate Healthcare, and Reproduce the Pivot

The raw table stays untouched. A SQL VIEW becomes the reusable cleaned layer that reporting tools can query.

Cleaning + Enriching + ValidatingContext before you run this exercise
SituationThe organization needs one reusable, documented definition of “clean retail data” without overwriting retail_raw.
Your jobPublish the cleaning logic as a SQL VIEW that deduplicates, standardizes, converts currency, imputes status, and flags missing Amount.
Checkpoint6 rows, 3 regions, T1003=$250, T1004 status=Standard, T1005 Missing_Amount=1.
Why it mattersA shared view gives downstream analysts and dashboards one consistent source of truth.
Create the cleaned retail view
CREATE VIEW retail_clean AS
SELECT DISTINCT
  Transaction_ID,
  Date,
  CASE
    WHEN Region IN ('NE','North East') THEN 'North East'
    WHEN Region IN ('S. Region','South') THEN 'South'
    ELSE Region
  END AS Region,
  Store_Type,
  ROUND(Amount * CASE WHEN Currency='GBP' THEN 1.25 ELSE 1.00 END, 2) AS Amount_USD,
  COALESCE(NULLIF(TRIM(Customer_Status),''),'Standard') AS Customer_Status,
  CASE WHEN Amount IS NULL THEN 1 ELSE 0 END AS Missing_Amount
FROM retail_raw;
Read it in English: Build a saved clean view: remove identical duplicate output rows, standardize Region labels, convert GBP to USD, replace missing/blank status with Standard, and keep a visible missing-amount flag.
CREATE VIEW
Save a query as a reusable logical table.
SELECT DISTINCT
Return identical selected rows once.
CASE ... END
Conditional recoding or calculation.
NULLIF(TRIM(x),'')
Trim spaces; turn empty text into NULL.
COALESCE(...,'Standard')
Return the first non-NULL value; use Standard when the cleaned status is missing.
IS NULL
Correct SQL test for missingness; do not write = NULL.
Expected: 6 rows · 3 regions · T1003 = 250.00 USD · T1004 = Standard · T1005 Missing_Amount = 1.
Cleaning + ValidatingContext before you run this exercise
SituationPatient data contains a duplicate, mixed gender codes, missing discharge reasons, and a discharge date before admission.
Your jobStandardize and impute documented categories while preserving a Date_Error flag for the impossible record.
CheckpointP001 is deduplicated, gender is standardized, P002/P005 become Standard, and P003 is flagged.
Why it mattersFlagging separates “record needs review” from “record should disappear,” which is crucial for clinical and audit accountability.
Healthcare clean view and logical date flag
CREATE VIEW healthcare_clean AS
SELECT DISTINCT
  Patient_ID, Admission_Date, Discharge_Date,
  CASE WHEN Gender IN ('M','Male') THEN 'Male'
       WHEN Gender IN ('F','Female') THEN 'Female'
       ELSE 'Unknown' END AS Gender,
  COALESCE(NULLIF(TRIM(Discharge_Reason),''),'Standard') AS Discharge_Reason,
  Cost,
  CASE WHEN Discharge_Date < Admission_Date THEN 1 ELSE 0 END AS Date_Error
FROM healthcare_raw;
Read it in English: Deduplicate patients, standardize the two gender coding systems, fill missing discharge reasons according to the exercise rule, and mark any encounter where discharge occurs before admission.
Expected: P003 gets Date_Error = 1.
Why a manager cares: Flagging preserves P003 for audit instead of silently destroying the record.
Publishing / ValidatingContext before you run this exercise
SituationThe audit view should retain P003, but a cost report should not treat an impossible encounter as verified evidence.
Your jobFilter Date_Error=0 for the managerial report, then aggregate average Cost by discharge reason.
CheckpointStandard=$3,050; Recovered=$4,200; Transfer=$6,100 for 5 valid encounters.
Why it mattersA report should state which records were excluded and why; otherwise a validation decision can silently change an average.
Report only verified encounters
SELECT Discharge_Reason,
       ROUND(AVG(Cost),2) AS Avg_Cost,
       COUNT(*) AS Patients
FROM healthcare_clean
WHERE Date_Error = 0
GROUP BY Discharge_Reason
ORDER BY Avg_Cost;
Read it in English: Keep valid encounters, group them by cleaned discharge reason, calculate average Cost, show the number of patients behind each average, and sort by average cost.
Expected: Standard 3050 (n=2) · Recovered 4200 (n=2) · Transfer 6100 (n=1).
Structuring + PublishingContext before you run this exercise
SituationManagers want the same region-by-store-type summary produced in Excel, but the data may live in a database.
Your jobUse GROUP BY plus conditional SUM expressions to reproduce the PivotTable structure in SQL.
CheckpointWest Coast=$420, South=$250, North East=$225.50; total=$895.50.
Why it mattersMatching the Excel checkpoint confirms that different tools implement the same business rules consistently.
SQL pivot using conditional aggregation
SELECT Region,
  SUM(CASE WHEN Store_Type='Brick-and-Mortar' THEN Amount_USD ELSE 0 END) AS Brick_and_Mortar,
  SUM(CASE WHEN Store_Type='Online' THEN Amount_USD ELSE 0 END) AS Online,
  SUM(COALESCE(Amount_USD,0)) AS Grand_Total
FROM retail_clean
GROUP BY Region
ORDER BY Grand_Total DESC;
Read it in English: Create one row per cleaned Region, send each sale into the appropriate store-type column, calculate the regional total, and show the largest region first.
Expected: West Coast 420 · South 250 · North East 225.50. Combined total = $895.50.
Cross-tool validation: This is the same total as the Excel PivotTable. Python must reproduce it next.

14. Outliers, Anomalies, Publishing & Governance

The attached Week 4 materials add an important warning: not every unusual value is an error, and cleaning is not finished until the rules, exceptions, and final output are documented.

Outlier ≠ error

An extreme salary, cost, or transaction may be a typo—but it may also be a real high-cost case, fraud signal, safety event, or important exception.

Over-cleaning is a risk

Automatically deleting anything unusual can remove the very evidence a manager most needs to investigate.

Context determines treatment

A value should be capped, corrected, excluded, or retained only when a documented business rule or domain expert supports that choice.

Publishing completes the workflow

Save or deliver the cleaned dataset with its assumptions, flags, and validation results so the next user knows what changed.

16. Managerial Judgment, Reliability, Validity & Ethics

Python will do exactly what you instruct it to do. The computer cannot decide whether your cleaning rule is fair, clinically appropriate, legally defensible, or conceptually valid. That responsibility remains with people.

Common Week 4 pitfalls: over-cleaning, ignoring context, and failing to document changes. A technically neat dataset can still be analytically wrong if the cleaning rule removes meaningful evidence or cannot be defended.

Missingness → Reliability

Deleting incomplete rows may systematically remove people with lower access, different language needs, or more complex circumstances.

Categories → Validity

Two labels that look similar may represent different concepts. Standardization must follow meaning, not appearance.

Outliers → Ethics

A rare observation may be an error, but it may also be a safety event, fraud signal, vulnerable group, or emerging need.

Documentation → Governance

Record what changed, why, by whom, using which rule, and how the output was validated.
